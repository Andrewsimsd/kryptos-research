# K4 current status and statement provenance

## Classical schedules, 21 September 2026

The [milestone 11 census](milestone-11.md) passed a fresh hidden-seed
calibration: 120/120 planted templates survived, 40/40 designed tamper cases
excluded their planted template, and an independent enumeration agreed exactly.
The gated K4 run checked 2,192 aligned standard-A–Z templates and retained 173:
12 repeating/interrupted, 152 progressive, and 9 autokey. These are
underdetermined compatibility classes; none is ranked as plaintext.

## Blind benchmark, 20 September 2026

The [milestone 10 reference benchmark](milestone-10.md), specifically the
fresh-seed BLIND-0004 run, passed its registered synthetic gate: 160/160 planted models ranked first, and 0/140 out-of-family
or tampered cases had a survivor. It tested a small exact family of period-one
and period-two standard-alphabet ciphers under identity or reverse order. It
did not search K4, calibrate language scoring, or close the physical and
period-clock gaps below. The earlier BLIND-0001 pilot published its seed, and
BLIND-0002's completed run replayed cases already exposed during a failed run.
BLIND-0003 had an incomplete implementation-path and final wall-cap gate.
Those runs remain auditable but do not count as the accepted calibration.
Future attack families need separate
representative corpora and blind tests.

## Stewardship and verification

[Steven Levy's 12 June 2026 WIRED report](https://www.wired.com/story/crypto-guys-bought-the-answer-to-the-cias-mysterious-kryptos-sculpture/)
identifies Paradigm as the purchaser and describes a hash-based checker charging
$1 per submission. It distinguishes K4 checking from separate prize puzzles.
These are reported launch details, not verified current operating terms
(S103; C102–C103, C121).

The announcement, K4 page, and CTF rules at Paradigm could not be read: web
retrieval failed and direct public HTTP reads returned 403. The CTF landing page
returned a title but no readable body. Consequently, this audit cannot establish
current normalization rules, checker availability, payment requirements, result
semantics, or later authenticated announcements. No submission or payment was
made. A successful plaintext check would still leave the cryptanalytic and
historical objectives separate (S104–S108; C104).

The [RR Auction primary lot record](https://www.rrauction.com/auctions/lot-detail/350761607302001-the-complete-secrets-of-kryptos-jim-sanborns-private-archive/)
lists a 20 November 2025 close and **$962,500 including buyer's premium**.
Its inventory describes plaintexts and coding material, but it expressly says
the auctioneer had not independently examined the secret documents. The lot
does not publish them. AP's $963,000 is a rounded reported figure; use the lot's
exact amount where precision matters (S109–S110; C101).

## Discovery and accessible archives

[Richard Byrne's firsthand account](https://stage-write.ghost.io/not-so-wonderful-things/)
dates his archive visit to 2 September 2025. He describes photographing material
for Jarett Kobek, who recovered the message using those photographs and public
clues. That account does not publish the message or recover the cipher method
(S111; C100).

The [Smithsonian finding aid](https://sirismm.si.edu/EADpdfs/AAA.sanbojim.pdf)
was updated on 6 May 2026. Its printed page 1 describes the collection as open
for research by appointment in Washington, D.C. Printed pages 8–9 list Kryptos
material in Box 6, folders 8–19, including preparation notes, cipher research,
correspondence, and chart material; additional Kryptos files appear in Box 18,
folders 10–13. These are catalog leads, not inspected contents (S112; C116).

This general access statement does not establish availability of the particular
plaintext-bearing scraps. The auction page says the archives were sealed for
50 years, while Byrne more narrowly describes restrictions on parts. The exact
restricted subset and any change in access remain unresolved. Do not infer that
all papers are inaccessible or that the secret scraps are presently open
(C117). The linked Smithsonian 2009 oral history was not retrievable (S113).

## Creator statements and fixed clues

The [August 2025 letter hosted by Elonka Dunin](https://www.elonka.com/kryptos/OpenLetterAug2025.html)
is accessible. It discusses auction plans, unsuccessful AI-generated submissions,
and continuation of the riddle as K5. Its explicit clue reads: “what's the point?
Power resides with a secret, not without it.” Preserve that wording as context,
not an additional plaintext crib (S100; C109–C110).

[Dan Vergano's firsthand 12 November 2025 report](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/cia-kryptos-puzzle-creator-releases-final-clues/)
attributes the World Clock identification, the 1986 Egypt journey, the 1989 Berlin
Wall event, and a message-delivery theme to Sanborn. It also describes a future
97-character K5. Its isolated “79 letters” reference is inconsistent with its own
later 97-character description and the established K4 transcript. Do not change
the baseline to 79. The article's linked original letter scan was located but
could not be fetched; its wording has not been independently inspected
(S101–S102; C105–C108, C120).

The 2014 [WIRED interview](https://www.wired.com/2014/11/second-kryptos-clue/)
confirms BERLIN at 64–69 and CLOCK immediately afterward. Its Set Theory Clock
discussion is historical context; the later explicit World Clock identification
controls the semantic referent (S114). The following chronology separates the

(S118; C115).

## Claims excluded from the baseline

[SolveKryptos's own solution page](https://solvekryptos.com/solution) says that
the unanchored characters are a reconstruction and that most of its lookup
values were fitted to that reconstruction. That makes its forward agreement a
construction check, not authentication. This audit neither imports its proposed
message nor treats its statement about an unreleased historical message as
independent evidence (S119; C118).
